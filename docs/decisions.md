# VeriLock Pi Version 1 — Engineering Decision Log

| Project phase | Decision | Alternatives and trade-offs | Rationale | Revisit condition |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Concept selection | Support facial recognition and RFID. | RFID-only, face-only, and mobile or wireless access. The weighted matrix records Concept A and Concept B. | The selected face-and-RFID concept scored **4.16/5** versus **3.28/5** for Concept B. Its security and ease-of-use scores outweighed Concept B’s cost advantage. | A change in access requirements or credential performance alters the balance of security, ease of use, and cost. |
| Authentication architecture | Process credentials and authorize access locally on the Raspberry Pi. | Cloud-hosted recognition adds a dependency on network and service availability. Local processing places data protection and recovery on the system owner. | On-device processing keeps entry available offline and keeps biometric processing on the Pi. | Central administration becomes a requirement and can be provided while preserving offline access and biometric privacy. |
| Mechanical prototype iteration | Use a rack servo for gear engagement and a separate servo for geared deadbolt rotation. | The earlier prototype used solenoid engagement. The team evaluated actuation options against torque, control of engagement and release, and mechanical complexity. | The geared drive provides bolt torque and disengages after powered movement so the original key can operate the deadbolt independently. | The drive fails to meet bolt travel, engagement, or manual key acceptance criteria. |
| Electrical integration | Power the Pi and servos from separate 5 V UPS branches with a shared signal ground. | A shared supply would place the controller and actuator loads on the same power branch. | Separate branches keep actuator load off the controller supply. The shared ground gives the Pi and servos a common PWM signal reference. | Supply stability, backup runtime, or power recovery falls outside the acceptance requirements. |
